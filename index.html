About

A program manager who ships complex transformations.

I run technical programs — the schedule, the risks, the stakeholders, the decisions. What I've learned, the hard way: programs don't fail from the top. They fail quietly — in misaligned assumptions, dependencies nobody mapped, and risks the room wasn't set up to raise. My job is to make those invisible things visible, early.

Today I'm at Applied Materials in Sunnyvale as Technical Program Manager II in the C&F Group — the group's first dedicated TPM, running the end-to-end program schedule, risk register, and executive status reporting for an advanced hardware development program. Day-to-day, that means driving critical-path closure across process engineering, mechanical engineering, and supplier teams, and establishing the operating cadence for design reviews, risk triage, and milestone gates.

Before Applied Materials, I spent a year at Amazon Robotics as a Technical Program Manager Co-op — governed the program schedule and RAID log across four enterprise transformation workstreams with zero missed milestones, drove an Oracle APEX approval dashboard to go-live that cut PO approval from 15–20 days to 1–2, and managed an engineering automation program that saved $1.7M annually. Phil Conlon called the work “freakin awesome” — which I'm choosing to count as a KPI.

Before Amazon, two years at Wipro as a Senior ERP Consultant — delivered three full-lifecycle implementations with zero Priority 1 incidents, governed approval workflows across six levels for a seven-subsidiary client (cut PO cycle 8 → 3 days, closed a 2-year government audit), and recovered $0.8M in early-payment discounts. Finished Purdue's MEM in May 2026 (GPA 3.7). PMP-certified, three SAP certifications, Microsoft Azure AZ-900. Outside work — I deadlift 400+, watch cricket whenever India plays, and have re-read Working Backwards three times now.

CurrentlyA few months into my role as Technical Program Manager II at Applied Materials in Sunnyvale. Not actively looking, but always happy to connect with other TPMs, program leaders, and anyone curious about the enterprise-systems-to-hardware bridge.
